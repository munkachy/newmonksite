Picking up the phone

Meditations

One monk's conversations with God in the Psalms

David composing the Psalms, Paris Psalter, about 960, Bibliothèque nationale de France, MS gr. 139 (photo: Gennadii Saus i Segura) · public domain

These are examples of one way to pray the Psalms. In each one, I hear one verse as my own voice and the next as God answering me. Every call is different: one day sorrowful, the next full of joy.

What kind of mood are you in right now?

Tap one, and you'll go straight to a call for that kind of day.

Stuck, distracted, or not sure it's working? Each meditation ends with a tip, and all the tips are on one page. There's also a page of favorite quotes about prayer.

Psalm numbers: the first is the number in most Bibles; the one in parentheses is the number in our choir book. Why there are two.

Please read this first

  • These are personal meditations by a Benedictine monk. They are not Church teaching, and they are not part of the Bible.
  • The psalm text is never changed. It is shown in plain type in the shaded boxes, in short excerpts from the Grail translation. That's the translation our choir sings, so these are the very words you'll hear in church.
  • The heard reply is set apart in italics, in the same box as its verse: beside it on a wide screen. On a phone, tap the box to open the meditation. A small picture shows who is speaking: an ancient icon of Christ for “I hear God say,” and a Benedictine monk in his black habit for “I pray.” It isn't a new translation, and it isn't a claim about what the psalm's writer meant. Hebrew poetry likes to speak in pairs, and the second line often deepens the first. That pairing simply invites us to listen for an answer.

A sorrowful call

Psalm 102 (101)

Interceding for the dying

O Lord, listen to my prayer
and let my cry for help reach you.

Psalm 102 (101):2

I hear God sayI'm listening to your prayer. Your cry has reached me. What do you ask of me?

Do not hide your face from me
in the day of my distress.
Turn your ear towards me
and answer me quickly when I call.

Psalm 102 (101):3

I prayYou did not hide your face from me when I was in distress. Now others are in distress, and I want to pray for them.

I hear God sayMy ear is turned toward you. I will answer you quickly.

For my days are vanishing like smoke,
my bones burn away like a fire.

Psalm 102 (101):4

I prayLook at the dying all over the world. Their days are vanishing like smoke.

My heart is withered like the grass.
I forget to eat my bread.

Psalm 102 (101):5

I hear God sayYes, my son. Some of them are so worn out from old age that they forget to eat.

I cry with all my strength
and my skin clings to my bones.

Psalm 102 (101):6

I prayGive them strength to cry out to you, even when their skin clings to their bones.

In this prayer I let some of the psalm's own “I” lines become God's voice, and I turn others into prayers for people who are dying. That is the freedom of a meditation. The psalm itself still says exactly what it says.

Tip

What if I get stuck?

Sometimes the line has static on it. You listen for God's answer, and nothing comes, or it comes through garbled. That's normal. Saint Paul says so:

For now we see in a mirror, dimly, but then face to face. Now I know in part, but then I will know fully, even as I was also fully known.

1 Corinthians 13:12, World English Bible

(The old King James Bible says we see “through a glass, darkly.”) In this life we really can talk with God, but not yet face to face. A little static comes with being a pilgrim.

That's the great gift of chanting together. When you get stuck, the choir keeps moving and carries you along to the next verse. So no big deal. Keep moving through the Psalms. Little by little they become part of you, and the line gets clearer. Some static will always be there until we see him face to face, and that's all right.

A joyful call

Psalm 92 (91)

Our community prays this psalm at Lauds on Saturday morning.

It is good to give thanks to the Lord,
to make music to your name, O Most High,

Psalm 92 (91):2

I hear God sayIt is good to give thanks to me, and to make music to my name. I am the Most High.

to proclaim your love in the morning
and your truth in the watches of the night,

Psalm 92 (91):3

I prayThen I will proclaim your love this morning, and your truth again tonight.

on the ten-stringed lyre and the lute,
with the murmuring sound of the harp.

Psalm 92 (91):4

I hear God sayI'm the one who gave you your love of music, my son. Every instrument you pick up, every song you're learning to make: play it all for me.

Your deeds, O Lord, have made me glad;
for the work of your hands I shout with joy.

Psalm 92 (91):5

I prayWhen the music comes out right, I know it's really you playing through me. It makes me so happy I could shout.

O Lord, how great are your works!
How deep are your designs!

Psalm 92 (91):6

I hear God sayYes. Aren't my works great? My designs are so deep that I can work right inside you.

One day I prayed Psalm 102 for the dying. Another day I prayed this one, full of joy. Same monk, same choir, a completely different call.

Tip

What if the psalm doesn't match my mood?

Some days the psalm is dancing and you feel like a wet rag. Other days you're happy and the psalm is weeping. Pray it anyway, for someone else. Somewhere, right now, someone feels exactly what this psalm feels. Lend them your voice. That's a big part of what monks do all day: we pray in the name of people who can't.

An honest call

Psalm 38 (37)

On a gloomy day, for no good reason. This was the first psalm I prayed this way.

My wounds are foul and festering,
the result of my own folly.

Psalm 38 (37):6

I prayLord, I'm gloomy today, and I don't even know why. I can't see any of the good things you've given me.

I am bowed and brought to my knees.
I go mourning all the day long.

Psalm 38 (37):7

I hear God sayI see you, my son, bent low before me, carrying this heaviness from morning to night. I am the Good Physician, and I'm going to help you get better.

All my frame burns with fever;
all my body is sick.

Psalm 38 (37):8

I prayThen heal me. I'm burning with a fever, the fever of ingratitude. It's like a fever dream: everything seems wrong, when really I'm surrounded by blessings.

Spent and utterly crushed,
I cry aloud in anguish of heart.

Psalm 38 (37):9

I hear God sayI know how worn out you are. Every cry of your heart reaches me.

O Lord, you know all my longing:
my groans are not hidden from you.

Psalm 38 (37):10

I prayYes, Lord, nothing in me is hidden from you. You've given me life and everything good, and still these feelings come. I'm not hiding them from you.

A gloomy mood isn't always ingratitude. Sometimes it is an illness, and that is neither a sin nor a sign of holiness. If sadness won't lift, please talk to a doctor or counselor as well as a priest. The Psalms can keep you company on that road too.

Tip

Do I have to feel something?

No. Feelings are like weather: they come and go. Some days prayer is warm, and some days it's as dry as dust. God doesn't measure your prayer by how it feels. Showing up is the prayer. Keep showing up.

When your own heart talks back

Psalm 42 (41)

Like the deer that yearns
for running streams,
so my soul is yearning
for you, my God.

Psalm 42 (41):2

I prayLord, I'm dry inside. I want you the way a worn-out animal wants water.

My tears have become my bread,
by night, by day,
as I hear it said all the day long:
“Where is your God?”

Psalm 42 (41):4

I hear God sayI see every tear, my son. People ask where I am, and some nights you ask it too. I am right here, closer to you than you are to yourself.

Why are you cast down, my soul,
why groan within me?
Hope in God; I will praise him still,
my savior and my God.

Psalm 42 (41):6

I prayThen why do I keep sinking? Come on, heart, wait for him. I'll be singing his praise again before long.

By day the Lord will send
his loving kindness;
by night I will sing to him,
praise the God of my life.

Psalm 42 (41):9

I hear God sayI'll send you my kindness every morning. And tonight, when you sing to me in choir, I'll be listening.

In this psalm the soul talks to itself: sinking, then rising. Sometimes the first voice that answers you is your own heart, remembering who God is. And then God answers too.

Tip

What if my mind wanders?

It will. Everyone's does, monks included. When you notice you've drifted, don't scold yourself. Just come back at the next verse. Every verse is a new door, and coming back is part of the prayer.

A call God finishes

Psalm 91 (90)

… says to the Lord: “My refuge,
my stronghold, my God in whom I trust!”

Psalm 91 (90):2

I prayYou are my safe place, Lord. I'm putting my whole weight on you.

When he calls I shall answer: “I am with you,”
I will save him in distress and give him glory.

Psalm 91 (90):15

I hear God sayCall me, and I'll pick up. Whatever trouble comes, you won't face it alone. I'm here right now, my son.

The psalm begins with the soul pledging its trust. At the end, God himself has the last word (verses 14–16).

Tip

How do I know it's God and not just me?

Honestly, you can't always be sure. These meditations aren't visions or new messages from heaven. They're a way of praying with the imagination God gave you. Here's a good test: Does what you hear agree with the Bible? Does it make you more loving, more humble, more at peace? Then keep listening. If it makes you proud, harsh, or afraid, it isn't from God. Let it go, and keep praying.

For anxious days

Psalm 46 (45)

God is for us a refuge and strength,
a helper close at hand, in time of distress,

Psalm 46 (45):2

I prayLord, I need you close today. Everything feels unsteady.

so we shall not fear though the earth should rock,
though the mountains fall into the depths of the sea;

Psalm 46 (45):3

I hear God sayEven if everything you lean on gives way, I won't. Don't be afraid.

“Be still and know that I am God,
supreme among the nations, supreme on the earth!”

Psalm 46 (45):11

I prayAll right, Lord. I'm putting the phone down. I'm turning off the news. I'm listening.

The Lord of hosts is with us:
the God of Jacob is our stronghold.

Psalm 46 (45):12

I hear God sayI walked with Jacob when he ran from home with nothing. I'm walking with you, too. I haven't left.

In verse 11, God himself speaks in the psalm. The soul's only job is to stop and listen.

Tip

How much should I pray?

Start small. One verse is enough. Read it slowly, listen, answer, and stop. A short prayer prayed with your whole heart is worth more than a long one rushed. You can always come back tomorrow.

Confession, and the answer

Psalm 32 (31)

I kept it secret and my frame was wasted.
I groaned all day long,

Psalm 32 (31):3

I prayLord, I hid it for so long. It ate away at me, day and night.

But now I have acknowledged my sins;
my guilt I did not hide.
I said: “I will confess
my offense to the Lord.”
And you, Lord, have forgiven
the guilt of my sin.

Psalm 32 (31):5

I hear God sayAnd the moment you told me, it was gone. No waiting room. No file.

I will instruct you and teach you
the way you should go;
I will give you counsel
with my eye upon you.

Psalm 32 (31):8

I prayThen lead me, Lord. Keep your eyes on me, and I'll follow where you look.

Tip

Can I tell God the hard stuff?

Yes. The Psalms do. They complain, they grieve, they ask God where he went. God put those prayers in the Bible on purpose. He would rather have your honest anger than your polite silence. Tell him what's really there. (Catholics can also bring their sins to confession and hear the words of forgiveness out loud.)

When you're afraid God is keeping score

Psalm 130 (129)

If you, O Lord, should mark our guilt,
Lord, who would survive?

Psalm 130 (129):3

I prayLord, if you're keeping score, I'm finished.

But with you is found forgiveness:
for this we revere you.

Psalm 130 (129):4

I hear God sayI'm not keeping score. I'm forgiving. That's who I am.

The dread in the first verse is real, but the second verse has the last word. And “revere” here means awe, the reverence a child has for a loving father, not terror. “There is no fear in love; but perfect love casts out fear” (1 John 4:18, World English Bible).

Tip

What if I'm not good enough to pray?

You're in good company. Many of the Psalms come down to us under the name of King David, who committed adultery and murder, then repented with his whole heart. The Psalms were written by sinners, for sinners. God doesn't wait for you to clean up before he picks up the phone. Come as you are. He does the cleaning.

The little way

Psalm 131 (130)

O Lord, my heart is not proud
nor haughty my eyes.
I have not gone after things too great
nor marvels beyond me.

Psalm 131 (130):1

I prayLord, I'm done trying to be big. I don't have to understand everything.

Truly I have set my soul
in silence and peace.
A weaned child on its mother’s breast,
even so is my soul.

Psalm 131 (130):2

I hear God sayThen come here and be quiet with me. Rest, like a little one asleep in its mother's arms.

The soul admits that it is small, and God gathers it up. This is Saint Thérèse's “little way” in two verses. Being small before God isn't a demotion. It's an embrace.

Tip

What about the quiet in between?

Silence is part of the conversation. Between verses, don't rush to fill the quiet. Sometimes God's answer isn't words at all, just peace, like a child resting in its mother's arms. In choir there's a short pause in the middle of every verse. Let it be a breath for listening.

Tip

What if I'm really tired?

Then make it simple. Boil the whole prayer down to one word. As you chant or read, listen for one short word from God, like love or God. One syllable is best. Hold on to that word. When your mind drifts, it brings you back. When you have people to pray for, tuck them all inside that one word and offer it up.

This comes from a mysterious little book called The Cloud of Unknowing. It was written in English in the late 1300s by someone who never signed it, and to this day nobody knows who the author was. The book teaches that one little word, like God or love, can carry your whole heart to God. For more than six hundred years it has quietly guided monks, nuns, and ordinary people.

It sounds almost too simple. It is simple. But it's powerful, and the longer you use it, the more you find inside that one word.

A grateful call

Psalm 116 (115 in the older numbering, which splits this psalm in two)

How can I repay the Lord
for his goodness to me?

Psalm 116 (115):12

I prayLord, you've given me so much. What could I ever give you back?

The cup of salvation I will raise;
I will call on the Lord’s name.

Psalm 116 (115):13

I hear God sayJust lift the cup I'm handing you, and call me by name.

I prayReally? That's all?

I hear God sayThat's all, my son.

We'd often rather do something heroic than something simple. But the cure for ingratitude is simple. Lift the cup. Say thank you.

Tip

How do I keep it going after prayer?

Take one line with you. Pick a verse, or an answer you heard, and carry it through the day. Say it while you wait in line, drive, or wash dishes. Monks have done this for centuries. It turns a whole day into a quiet conversation.

A call to praise

Psalm 147 (146)

Our community prays this psalm at Vespers on Saturday evening.

Praise the Lord for he is good;
sing to our God for he is loving:
to him our praise is due.

Psalm 147 (146):1

I hear God sayLittle one, I am the deepest reality there is. Know, deep down, that I am good. When you praise me, you say yes to what is most real. That is why something in you will always want to sing, even when you suffer.

The Lord builds up Jerusalem
and brings back Israel’s exiles,

Psalm 147 (146):2

I prayTruly, you build me up. You are building your Church, the new Jerusalem. I am an exile and a pilgrim, but I am on my way home, and every minute brings me closer to you.

he heals the broken-hearted,
he binds up all their wounds.

Psalm 147 (146):3

I hear God sayI see that you are sometimes broken-hearted. The world you live in is full of sharp edges. Many of these are growing pains, and growing always hurts. I will bind up your wounds.

He fixes the number of the stars;
he calls each one by its name.

Psalm 147 (146):4

I prayThe stars in the sky make me think of the saints. You call each of us by name, and you made us to live with you in heaven as saints. But how will you do this for me?

Our Lord is great and almighty;
his wisdom can never be measured.

Psalm 147 (146):5

I hear God sayI can do it because I am great and almighty. There is nothing I cannot do. You will never measure the depths of my wisdom, or understand them.

Praise is not pretending that everything is fine. It is telling the truth about who God is, even on a hard day.

Tip

What if I don't feel like praising?

Praise doesn't have to wait for good feelings. The Psalms praise God in exile, in sickness, and in the middle of the night. When you praise God on a hard day, you aren't saying the pain isn't real. You are saying that God is good even now, and that the pain won't have the last word. Start with one line. The feeling often follows.

Two voices in one verse

Psalm 27 (26)

O Lord, hear my voice when I call;
have mercy and answer.

Psalm 27 (26):7

I prayLord, are you there? Please answer me.

Of you my heart has spoken:
“Seek his face.”

Psalm 27 (26):8a

I hear God sayI'm the one who put that longing in your heart. Come closer. Look for me.

It is your face, O Lord, that I seek;

Psalm 27 (26):8b

I prayThen I'm looking, Lord. Don't turn away from me. Let me see you.

One verse, two voices: God's invitation, then the heart's answer. (Many Bibles put the invitation straight into God's mouth: “You said, ‘Seek my face.’”) God has already made the call. The only question left is how your heart will answer.

Tip

Do I have to do this alone?

No, and it's easier together. In choir you don't carry the psalm by yourself. One side sings, the other answers, and the whole church moves as one. Come and pray with us. Your presence helps carry us, and we will help carry you. See the prayer times.

Try it yourself

This is not a technique with steps. It's more like a style, a way of listening that becomes your own. Pick a psalm. Read one verse as your voice. Then read the next, and listen for how God might be saying it to you. Don't change the words. Just listen.

And when you're ready, come and pray them with us.
