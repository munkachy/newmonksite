# Monastery websites: what to learn for St. Bernard Abbey

Research done 1 Nov 2026. Screenshots are in `shots/`: `<site>-desktop.png` (1440 wide) and `<site>-phone.png` (390 wide), cut off at 6000 px. Extracted text is in `text/`.
Sites studied: St. Michael's Abbey (with its community page, menu and The Abbot's Circle), Subiaco (the chooser page plus the abbey and academy sites), Clear Creek, Saint Meinrad, Saint Louis Abbey (with Priory School), Portsmouth Abbey (monastery and school), Mount Angel, Christ in the Desert, Gethsemani, Belmont, Conception and Saint Vincent.
**Norcia** (osbnorcia.org, norciabeer.com) could not be reached from this network, so it is left out.

A note on the screenshots: several sites (St. Michael's, Saint Meinrad, Clear Creek, Portsmouth School) fade content in as you scroll. In a full-page capture those sections come out as large blank bands. That tells us something real: any reader whose browser doesn't run the animation sees the same blank space (see Pitfalls).

## 1. St. Michael's Abbey (Norbertines, Silverado CA): stmichaelsabbey.com

Shots: `stmichaels-desktop/phone.png`, `stmichaels-menu.png`, `stmichaels-community-desktop.png`, `stmichaels-abbotscircle-desktop.png`. Built on Webflow by the agency R/nD.

**Home page, top to bottom**
1. **Hero:** a full-bleed **still photograph**, not a video. Canons in white habits walk away from the camera, out of focus, down the nave toward a gold apse mosaic and crucifix. The reader seems to walk in the procession. "St. Michael's Abbey" is set very large (about 100 px) in Voyage, a thin, high-contrast display serif, and fades in. Under it are three plain lines: "A community of Canons Regular of Premontre, under the guardianship of St. Michael the Archangel… dedicated for over 60 years to daily liturgical prayer, teaching, and service." No buttons sit on the hero. A cream card in the lower-right corner reads **"Daily Public Prayer Times / Pray with us. / See the schedule >"**.
2. **Header:** logo shield on the left. In the middle, a white pill shows the hours: **"Visitors' Center: M-F 9:00 a.m. – 4:30 p.m."**. Then "Contact & Directions", "Prayer Schedule", an outlined **♥ Donate** pill, and a hamburger in a gold square. That's all.
3. **Upcoming Events:** a small chapel photo beside a large gold "Upcoming Events" heading. The list is typed by hand in italics ("Sunday, November 1 – Feast of All Saints / Regular Sunday Mass Schedule 7:00, 9:00, 12:00"), followed by two buttons: "See all upcoming events" and "See daily prayer schedule".
4. **Three asks, each with a photo** (they stack on the phone):
   - "Our vocation is to pray. The community's prayers are our greatest gift." → **Submit a prayer intention**
   - "Discern God's call. Become a living sacrifice…" → **Learn more**
   - "Be part of our mission." → **Join the Abbot's Circle**

   Prayer comes first, then vocations, then money, and that order is deliberate.
5. **The Abbot's Circle band:** a dark black-and-white photo of a hand drawing. "The Abbot's Circle is a first-of-its-kind virtual monastery: an evolving, curated library… inside look at the artistic, cultural, intellectual, and spiritual work…" A ghost circle button says **Enter**.
6. **"Immersed in the 900-year tradition of our order…":** one paragraph in a large serif, with two arrow links (About Us, Vocations) and a photo of two priests at the altar among gold candlesticks.
7. Newsletter sign-up ("Stay up-to-date with the Abbey… receive inspiring content!") and a black footer with office hours, phone, email and address.

**Navigation:** the hamburger opens a full-screen dark overlay over a blurred photo. It has only three huge serif items: **About Us ▾ / Ministries ▾ / Vocations**. "Connect" (social icons) and "Visit The Abbot's Circle" sit on the right. Under Ministries: Archangel Institute, Summer Camp, Request a Mass, Retreats, Let Us Pray For You, Lay Order, Brotherhood of St. Dismas. Three items is a very small, confident menu.

**The canons themselves:** the Community Members page is a wall of matching studio portraits: each man in white habit, in front of the same dark bookshelf, shot in the same light. There are about 100 of them. The abbot and priors come first in larger cards, then a ✠ rule, then every priest, then the seminarians. Each card gives name, office, **Feast Day** and **Hometown** ("Fr. Gabriel Stack, Feast Day: February 27th, Hometown: Kennet, MO"). Seen together, the sheer number makes the point. The cards carry no stories, though.

**Daily life and prayer:** the `/hours-schedule` page is a clean two-column timetable, Monday–Saturday and Sunday, from 5:45 Matins & Lauds to 8:30 Compline. It footnotes what a "Conventual Mass" is and adds "The abbey church will be locked immediately after Compline at 8:50 p.m.". Confession times follow.

**History:** one paragraph on the About page. The Vocations page has a short life of St. Norbert. There is no timeline.

**What they make or sell:** nothing is sold on the main site. The "product" is **The Abbot's Circle** (theabbotscircle.com), a membership and content site. It has a dark cinematic hero ("Welcome to The Abbot's Circle"), "Special upcoming events: The Abbatial Blessing, Michaelmas, & more", a sideways-scrolling "Featured Posts" row ("The Newly Ordained, 2026"; "St. Michael's Abbey Elects First New Abbot in More Than 30 Years"), and categories: Sacred Art, Sacred Music, Abbey Life, Spirituality, Formation. Its taglines: "A rich digital library / A center for holiness / A place of art & music". It is giving and storytelling dressed as a magazine.

**School:** St. Michael's Prep has closed. All that remains is a line, "For Prep School Transcripts, please contact transcripts@…", under Ministries. The Archangel Institute replaces it: Monday-evening religion courses for boys 14–18, "the core religion curriculum at St. Michael's Prep", with fees, a course list and book lists. The Summer Camp page is excellent: the 2026 weeks are all marked "Sold Out", there is "A Typical Camp Day" timetable (6:30 Wake Up … 4:00 Nationball (A Camp Favorite) … 7:00 Stink Bug Hunt & Race … 8:00 Campfire!), and it closes with "campers play hard and pray hard!".

**Giving:** the Donate pill sits in the header on every page. `/one-time-donation` is a short paragraph ("an investment in the future of the Church") above an embedded **Donorbox** form, with a gifts@ email. The ongoing giving brand is "Join the Abbot's Circle".

**Vocations:** "Heeding the Call", St. Norbert's story, then "Come and See". It describes what a visitor does: pray the Office, attend classes with the novices, manual labour, meals in the refectory, "sports, art, hikes, board games". It lists frank criteria (ages 20–27, two years of college or work, Catholic for three years if a convert, **no debt**) and the full curriculum from postulancy through theology, then a JotForm.

**Guests/retreats:** one-day Saturday retreats, each with its preacher, a blurb and an hour-by-hour plan (7:00 Mass → Terce → Conferences → Sext → Tour → Confession), "$50", plus an invitation to stay for Vespers.

**News:** none on the main site. News lives on The Abbot's Circle.

**Typography and colour:** Voyage display serif for headings, very large and thin; the system font for body text. Palette: warm off-white `#f0eee9`, black, and a single mustard-gold accent (`~#e5a83b`) for headings, the menu square and the social icons. There's a lot of space between sections.

**Photography:** cinematic and shot by professionals. Shallow depth of field, warm tungsten light, faces often turned away or in profile, gold vessels in the foreground. Portraits are uniform studio shots. Nothing looks like a snapshot.

**What is clever or moving**
- The procession hero puts you in the line of canons.
- The asks are worded humbly: "Our vocation is to pray. The community's prayers are our greatest gift." The prayer-request box comes before the donation box.
- The visitors' hours pill in the header answers the most common phone call.
- Feast days on every portrait invite people to pray for a named priest on his day.

**What is weak**
- Scroll-triggered fade-ins leave large empty bands when animation doesn't run (they show as blank in the screenshots).
- The events are typed in by hand and will go stale.
- Gold text on cream has low contrast.
- The two footers disagree ("© 2024" in the menu, "© 2026" at the bottom).
- The 100-portrait wall has no stories.
- News, the shop-like content and giving all sit on a second domain, so the main site feels thin.
- No map or "how to find us" on the home page.

## 2. Subiaco Abbey & Academy (Arkansas, Benedictine; boarding school for boys): subi.org

Shots: `subiaco-desktop.png` (the chooser), `subiaco-abbey-*`, `subiaco-academy-*`. This is the **closest structural match to St. Bernard**: a Benedictine abbey that runs a boarding school for boys. Built on Finalsite.

**Chooser page:** subi.org is a split screen. The left half is a school group photo washed in navy with the **Subiaco Academy** crest and "Explore the Academy ›". The right half shows two monks in a cloister with **Subiaco Abbey** and "Explore the Abbey". Hovering over a half widens it. You must choose before seeing anything else.

**Twin sites:** abbey.subi.org and academy.subi.org use **the same template section for section**, so the family resemblance is obvious:

| Section | Abbey | Academy |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | aerial drone shot of the bell tower; "A Place of Hope in the Modern World" | boys at an outdoor Mass; "Growing Boys Graciously To Manhood / In The Benedictine Tradition Since 1887" |
| Quote strip | Rule of St. Benedict, Prologue ("a school of the Lord's service…") | a 2025 graduate: "Subiaco showed me what it means to be a genuine follower of Christ…" |
| "We Are Subiaco" number carousel | 2,000 years of Christianity / 1,500 years of Benedictine monasticism / 148 years in Arkansas / 30 monks | Ranked #1 private school in Arkansas / 1,600+ acres / 9:1 / 95% college enrolment |
| "Our Stories" photo strip | **"Our Subiaco Calling": A Tailor / An Iconographer / A Scholar / A Teacher / A Gardener**, each a monk photographed at his work | **"Men of Impact": A Leader / An Artist / A Veterinarian / A Doctor / A Surgeon**, alumni |
| "Four Pillars" accordion | Prayer / Work / Lectio / Brotherhood | Faith / Scholarship / Character / Brotherhood |
| Social | Instagram feed "A Glimpse into our Life" (@countrymonks: a monk at World Series Game 1, monks laughing in the kitchen) | Instagram feed and college logos |

**Navigation:**
- Abbey utility bar: Prayer Request / Become a Monk / Visit Us / Online Store / Support Us. The hamburger opens About Us (Our Monastery, Our Monks, Our Abbot, Meet our Monks, Recent Deaths, Oblates, Safeguarding), Discernment, Pray With Us, Work (Monastery, Education, Hospitality), Visit (Day Trip, Retreats, Tours), Support (Annual Appeal, Planned Giving, Mass Intentions, Tribute and Memorial Gifts, Columbarium, All Souls Prayer List).
- Academy utility bar: Apply / Inquire / Visit / Support Us / Online Store.

**Monks:** "Meet our Monks" opens with "Contrary to popular opinion, we monks are not all the same." There's a card for each of about 30 monks, and the ones who agreed have a "Read More" story about how God called them to Subiaco. "Our Daily Life" walks through the day by the bells ("The first bell rings at 6:20 a.m. giving the monks a ten-minute notice…").

**School and monastery together:** the abbey site has a "Work → Education" page telling the school's history from the monks' side: founded 1887, burned in 1901 and again in 1927, reopened each time. It states plainly that the Academy is owned by the monastic corporation. The two sites share a footer ("The Abbey Bells is our monthly newsletter for our Abbey, Academy, and Retreat Center") and one store.

**Giving:** the "Abbey Annual Appeal" copy is the best on any site studied: "WHAT DOES IT TAKE TO RUN A MONASTERY? … A monastery is not just a building. It is a way of life. When you give… you help keep the bells ringing, lights on, air conditioning running, meals prepared, monks cared for, guests welcomed, students formed, and the Hill ready to serve." It also offers a **Columbarium** and an **All Souls Prayer List** as ways to give.

**Typography and colour:** Garamond Premier for headings and body, Proxima Nova for small caps labels. Deep navy/indigo `#2b2d5c`-ish washes, with a gold "❝" and gold dots. Thin vertical lines run down the page like architectural guides.

**Clever:** the parallel "A Tailor / An Iconographer…" and "A Leader / An Artist…" strips say "the monks form men" without stating it. Calling the place **"the Hill"** gives a shared name for school, monks and alumni.

**Weak:**
- The chooser is an extra click, and a visitor looking for the gift shop or a retreat has to guess a side.
- The carousels hide most of the numbers.
- The home pages are generic Finalsite: the same layout could be any school.
- "Opens in new window/tab" appears all over the menus.

## 3. Our Lady of Clear Creek Abbey (Solesmes Benedictines, Oklahoma): clearcreekmonks.org

Shots: `clearcreek-desktop/phone.png`, `clearcreek-shop-*`. WordPress with WooCommerce.

**Home page:**
- A wide landscape photo: the stone abbey rising out of autumn Ozark forest. Under it, "Our Lady of Clear Creek Abbey" in **Roman Uncial**, a medieval-looking display face, then two paragraphs of plain fact ("founded in 1999 from… Fontgombault… currently counts 55 monks… ordered towards contemplation"), then "Experience More".
- A dark green band of three photos: a monk at a woodworking bench, a carved Madonna in a niche, a monk in a straw hat sowing a field.
- **Horarium:** a horizontal dial/slider of the day (Rise 4:50 → Matins 5:15 → … → Compline 8:25 → Rest). **It jumps to the current time and says "9:00 PM CST, Current Activity: Rest"**, then quotes the Rule: "Let them sleep clothed and girded… that they may be always ready" (RB 22). Every hour has its own Rule quote. This was the most charming idea found.
- "Latest" (Construction Update; Refectory Reading; a homily).
- A full-width photo of monks in black habits and straw hats on their knees planting.
- **"Ora et Labora"** essay with **an inline audio player: "Listen to a sample of our Gregorian Chant here"**, an Offertory antiphon with its Latin and English, and a closing line that they live from their land and workshops but "are dependent however on the generosity of benefactors to complete the construction of the Abbey."
- **"Some of our monastic products"**: a WooCommerce product grid (rosaries $19–29, icons), right on the home page.
- "Origo / History and Origins": the founding story told as chapters ("The Integrated Humanities Program" at the University of Kansas → "Blossoming of Vocations at Fontgombault" → "Long-Awaited Foundation in America" → "Monastic Life begins at Clear Creek" → "Cowboy Bethel…").

**Navigation:** Home / Abbey / Becoming a Monk / Latest / Visit / Media / **Gift Shop** / Contact / **Donate** / cart / search. The Gift Shop mega-menu is huge (Books with 20 subcategories, Candles, Coffee, Crosses, Nativity Scenes, **Refectory Readings**…).

**Clever:**
- **"Refectory Readings"** sells the book the monks are currently hearing read at lunch: "At the midday meal in the refectory, the monks are reading… *In the Steps of St. Paul*". That ties shopping to their real daily life.
- Construction updates make giving concrete ("the large construction crane… to raise the abbatial church to the skies").
- The tagline "Building Something Beautiful For God – To Last A Thousand Years" runs over every footer.

**Giving:** a Gravity Forms page on their own site with a monthly checkbox, PayPal, and a DAF/IRA/will note. The motto "to last a thousand years" carries the appeal.

**Typography and colour:** Roman Uncial headings and Work Sans body on a warm parchment `#fdf8f3`, with dark forest green `#1f3324` bands and small green links. The photography is excellent and documentary: real work, real weather, habits muddy at the knee.

**Weak:**
- The home page is 17,000 px long.
- The shop mega-menu swamps the site.
- Product images are lazy-loaded and empty until scrolled.
- Justified body text in small grey type.
- No faces or names of individual monks anywhere (by design: hidden life).

## 4. Saint Meinrad Archabbey (Indiana; seminary, publishing, caskets): saintmeinrad.org

Shots: `saintmeinrad-desktop/phone.png`.

**Home:**
- A hero photo, washed almost white, of monks in black processing with incense. On top sits a red line drawing of the twin-towered church with two black ravens (St. Meinrad's ravens), "SAINT MEINRAD", and **"We are 60+ monks living, working, and praying in a Benedictine monastery."** One button: **Learn about the monastery**.
- Then a stack of bands, each one heading + sentence + red pill button: **Watch Live** ("Mass and Vespers daily… View video options", a BoxCast stream), **Time for a Retreat**, **Connect with Us** (four outline tiles: Request a Mass Intention / Share a Prayer Request / On the Hill Newsletter / Echoes from the Bell Tower Podcast, plus a sign-up box), **Visit Us**, **Is God calling you?** ("…moving a little apart in order to seek God…"), **News & Events** in two columns, **Support Us** ("How can I help?").

**Navigation:** Monastery / Vocations / Visit / Retreats / Oblates / Connect / Support / **Works ▾**. The "Works" menu leads straight to its businesses and apostolates: Seminary & School of Theology, **Abbey Caskets**, **CareNotes**, **Tailor Shop**, **Peanut Brother** (the monks' peanut butter). Calling the businesses **"Works"** keeps the shop monastic in spirit: it is the *labora* of *ora et labora*.

**Giving:** Support goes to the seminary's site, which has an amount box, "Why give?" in three themes ("Monastic witness to a busy world"), and "Stories of impact" quotes from named monks and students. Ways to give: One-time / Monthly / Matching / Stock / Planned.

**Typography and colour:** Gambetta serif (all-caps headings with wide spacing) and Commissioner sans. Warm stone `#edeae6`, brick red buttons, and gold for the vocations button. A faint repeating raven pattern sits behind the bands.

**Weak:**
- The live stream embed leaves a large blank space.
- The "Monk Directory" link goes to a 404 (`/the-monastery/monks/`).
- Giving sends you off to the .edu site.
- Every band has the same weight, so nothing leads.

## 5. Saint Louis Abbey (Benedictine; runs Saint Louis Priory School): stlouisabbey.org

Shots: `stlouisabbey-desktop/phone.png`. Next.js on Vercel. The school has its own site, priory.org.

**Home:**
- A dark hero photo of a priest elevating the chalice, with a large gold **St. Benedict medal** emblem beside "Welcome to Saint Louis Abbey", the heading "Come and Seek God" and two buttons, **Learn more / Visit**.
- A cream section, "A School of the Lord's Service", with three icon bullets: Finding peace in silence with God / Unifying our work and prayer / Embracing a life of love and obedience.
- A **Liturgy Schedule** set out as four columns (Mon–Thurs / Friday / Saturday / Sunday) in 24-hour monastic time ("0540- Rise, 0600- Matins… 2200- Grand Silence").
- **Events** as a calendar list (Stained Glass: Christmas Ornaments; Calligraphy & Illumination; Weekly Holy Hour for Vocations).
- **"Ora et Labora – Prayer and Work"** in three photo cards: **Teaching Students with Patience** (a monk in a classroom), Living the Radical Witness of a Monastic Life, Serving God's People through Pastoral Work.
- A visitor's quote: "We were blown away by the Mass, the reverence, the homilies, and the wisdom and genuine joy of the monks."
- Monastery Gallery.

**Navigation:** About ▾ (Our Charism, Our Community, History, Parish, **School → priory.org**, Gallery) / Vocations ▾ (Am I called?, Schedule a visit, **A Day in the life**, The Process, Postulants & Novices) / Affiliates / **The Abbot's Corner** / **Sacred Art Institute**, with Support and Contact as buttons.

**The monks:** "Meet Our Family" is the best monk page found. Each monk has a portrait, a short life, and **two or three questions in his own words**:
- Abbot Thomas (once "a committed atheist") answers "What advice do you have for discernment?" with "I'm not a fan of the word discernment… pick a Gospel… read the whole thing."
- Prior Cuthbert ("raised protestant… attended Saint Louis Priory School") answers "Stop talking. A lot of young men 'discerning' are really having a dialogue with themselves and their fear."

**"A Day in the Life of Father Athanasius, Kitchen Master, Guest Master, and Monastic Bursar"** follows one monk through his day, including coaching the students' soccer team.

**School link:** the school is one item under About and has its own site. On the abbey side, the school appears as a *work of the monks* ("Teaching Students with Patience"). priory.org in turn puts "Monks of Saint Louis Abbey Announce Re-Election of Abbot…" in its own news. The prior was once a Priory student, and that story is told.

**Sacred Art Institute:** classes taught by monks and lay artists in stained glass, calligraphy and illumination, mosaic and icons, presented as a living continuation of Br. Symeon's windows and mosaics on the campus. This is the closest parallel to a Br. Joseph Zoettl legacy.

**Typography and colour:** Malko display serif and a system body font. Cream `#f3eedd`, near-black and brass-brown buttons.

**Weak:**
- 24-hour times ("1730") puzzle lay visitors.
- The dates in the events list are tiny.
- Content pages have little photography.

## 6. Portsmouth Abbey (Rhode Island; co-ed boarding school): portsmouthabbeymonastery.org and portsmouthabbey.org

Shots: `portsmouth-monastery-desktop.png` (the phone shot captured only the first screen, because the page scrolls inside a container), `portsmouth-school-*`.

**Two separate sites, two separate brands.**

The **monastery** site (eCatholic) has:
- A gold utility bar (About Us / Contact Us / Vocations / **School** / Donation).
- The main menu: Home / About Us / **The Current** / Monks / Get Inspired / Come and See / Vocations.
- A sunset hero of the modernist abbey church.
- The next three services in large type (**Sunday Mass 9:30 am ET / Daily Mass 7:20 am ET / Vespers 5:30 pm ET**) and a "Welcome Note" card.
- Live and Last Featured YouTube embeds.
- "Learn more about who we are": a paragraph of links to Monks, Preaching & Teaching, Abbey Classics, In Memoriam, Poetry & Prose.
- A "Benedictine Vocation" band with the first line of the Rule, then News, Prayer Requests, and Lectio Divina / Vocation Inquiry tiles.

**Monks page:** each monk has a quotation and a list of roles. Some are serious and some are playful: "Abbot, Chancellor of Abbey School, Director of Spiritual Life, & Theology Teacher, **Anseriform & Psittacine Aviculturist, Ethnomusicologist**"; "Prior, Guest master, **Environmentalist**, Cantor". There's a long **In Memoriam** with a page for each departed monk.

**"The Current"** is a weekly newsletter with recurring columns ("**This Week in Heaven**" for the saints of the week, "Artists of the Abbey", "Voices", "Places"). A regular voice like this is easy to keep up.

The **school** site (portsmouthabbey.org) opens on:
- a cookie banner,
- a **"CLICK HERE TO GIVE!" pop-up over the hero video** (a fiscal-year match appeal that was out of date by November),
- a chat bubble ("Hi, how can I help you today?").

The navigation shows internal pages called "SG – Athletics", "Style-Guide-and-Template" and "Campaign Leadership – old". It's a cautionary example.

**Weak:**
- The two sites share nothing visually.
- The monastery site looks dated (gold blocks, small type).
- The school site is cluttered and leaks its working pages.

## 7. Mount Angel Abbey (Oregon; seminary, library, guesthouse, brewery): mountangelabbey.org

Shots: `mountangel-desktop/phone.png`.

**Top navigation is a list of the abbey's institutions:** MONASTERY / SEMINARY / LIBRARY / GUESTHOUSE / (logo) / BREWERY / JOIN US / GIVING / VISIT US. On a hilltop with several "works", this shows the shape of the place at a glance.
- MONASTERY ▾ includes **Marketplace**, Prayer Schedule, Livestreaming, Abbot Jeremy, Talks & Homilies, Art Collection, **Iconography**.
- VISIT US ▾ includes Walking Tour Map, **Monastery Bells**, Abbey Pipe Organs, Abbey Museum, Bookstore & Coffeehouse.

**Home:**
- A carousel inside a framed box (the current slide: "Mount Angel Seminary Benefit Dinner").
- "Spiritual Resources at Mount Angel" in three portrait cards: Abbot Jeremy (Books, Homilies, & Videos) / Events & Retreats / Mount Angel Institute.
- For each institution, a small **gold line-art emblem** (a stained-glass-style icon), a title in Cormorant Garamond, a paragraph, and photo cards:
  - Monastery: **"Come Pray With Us"** and **"What Do Monks Do?"**, with the quote "If I am a monk, that means that I am just a man. I am searching, like we all do."
  - Guesthouse: **"Come as an Individual / Come as a Group / Come for the Day"**.
  - Seminary: an autumn photo of the quad.

**Typography and colour:** Cormorant Garamond throughout, warm grey `#efedea` panels, muted gold `#a8915f`. Restrained and elegant. Photography is warm and seasonal.

**Shop:** the "Marketplace" (free shipping over $60, "Your purchase supports the work of Mount Angel Abbey & Seminary"). Benedictine Brewery has a top-level link of its own.

**Weak:**
- The emblems and sections repeat until the page feels like a brochure.
- The carousel hero leads with a fundraising dinner rather than the monks.
- Very small grey body text.

## 8. Monastery of Christ in the Desert (New Mexico): christdesert.org

- Slideshow dots for 14 slides.
- A large white gap at the bottom of the page.

## 10. Belmont Abbey (North Carolina; Belmont Abbey College): belmontabbey.org

Shots: `belmont-desktop/phone.png`. The home page is almost entirely **a vocations funnel**.
- Hero: "Take Your Place in the 1,500-Year Benedictine Tradition" over the organ, with a large faint St. Benedict medal.
- A green band, "Let Us Help You Discern Your Vocation", with **three numbered steps**: 1. Take the questionnaire, 2. Speak to our Vocations Director, 3. Come and visit the Abbey.

# Synthesis for St. Bernard Abbey

St. Bernard is unusual: its two public faces are **the Ave Maria Grotto** (125 miniatures by one monk, Br. Joseph Zoettl, a pilgrim and tourist destination with a gift shop) and **St. Bernard Prep** (a boarding and day school). The abbey itself, the monks and their prayer, connects the two. Most visitors will come for the Grotto or the school. The site's job is to show them that both are works of one praying community.

## Ideas worth borrowing (15)

1. **A hero that puts you inside the monks' prayer** *(St. Michael's)*. One full-bleed photograph (or a short, silent, looping clip of Vespers) taken from among the monks in choir or procession, faces mostly turned away, warm light. Use one line of plain description under the name ("A community of Benedictine monks in Cullman, Alabama, praying, working and teaching since 1891") and no carousel.
2. **A "what are the monks doing now?" horarium** *(Clear Creek)*. A strip of the day's hours that highlights the current one in Central time ("2:15 PM: Work. Next: Vespers at 5:00") with the Rule verse for that hour. It's cheap to build in JavaScript and immediately memorable.
3. **Practical facts at the gate** *(St. Michael's header pill; Christ in the Desert's notice card)*. Put "Grotto open today 9–5 · Last admission 4:30 · Gift shop open" in the header or under the hero, driven by a small hours table that handles holidays and weather closures. It answers the question behind most phone calls.
4. **Twin sections with matching layouts** *(Subiaco)*. Give the Grotto, the School and the Abbey the same section pattern (hero, quote strip, "by the numbers", stories strip, pillars), so they look like one family, but **on one site, without Subiaco's chooser page**. Visitors should never have to pick a side before they see anything.
5. **Monks shown by their work** *(Subiaco "Our Subiaco Calling: A Tailor / An Iconographer / A Scholar / A Teacher / A Gardener")*. For St. Bernard: *A Teacher / A Dorm Dean / A Beekeeper / A Cantor / A Grotto Keeper*. Each is a monk photographed at the job, linking to his story. The school version uses the same strip for alumni ("A Pilot / A Priest / A Surgeon…").
6. **Monk pages with questions answered in their own words** *(Saint Louis Abbey "Meet Our Family")*. Give each monk a portrait, a short life, and two or three answers ("What is the hardest part of monastic life?"). Add **feast day and hometown** to each card *(St. Michael's)* so readers can pray for him on his day. Shoot all portraits in **one matching setup** (same backdrop and light), as St. Michael's did.
7. **"A Day in the Life of Br. ___"** *(Saint Louis Abbey, Fr. Athanasius)*. Pick a monk who teaches at the Prep and also tends the Grotto or the bells, and follow him from Vigils to Compline. It explains how school and monastery share one life better than any diagram.
8. **Call the shop and businesses "Works"** *(Saint Meinrad's "Works" menu)*. Put the Grotto gift shop, any abbey-made goods and the Prep under a heading like "Our Works", so commerce reads as *labora*.
9. **Sell what connects to daily life** *(Clear Creek "Refectory Readings")*. Offer "What the monks are reading at table this month", the Grotto guidebook, prints of Br. Joseph's miniatures, and a chant recording. Show **three products on the home page, no more**, with a link to the store.
10. **Chant you can hear right away** *(Clear Creek, Christ in the Desert)*. Put a small inline audio player near the prayer section: "Hear the monks sing the Salve Regina". No autoplay.
11. **Giving copy that names real costs** *(Subiaco "keep the bells ringing, lights on…"; Clear Creek's construction updates; Christ in the Desert's Perpetual Enrollment certificate)*. For example: "Your gift keeps the Grotto's 125 shrines repaired, the boys' dorms warm, and the bells ringing for Vespers." Offer one gift that comes with something to keep, such as a **"Friend of the Grotto" enrollment with a print of a miniature**, and a named giving circle like St. Michael's **Abbot's Circle**.
12. **Asks in humble order: prayer first** *(St. Michael's)*. The three home-page invitations in this order: "Let us pray for you" (a prayer request), "Is God calling you?" (vocations), "Be part of our mission" (giving). Keep a **♥ Donate** pill in the header on every page.
13. **A clear three-step vocations path** *(Belmont)*: 1. Read and pray, 2. Talk with the vocations director, 3. Come and see. Add St. Michael's honest criteria list and "what a come-and-see visit is like".
14. **"In [year]…" community numbers** *(Saint Vincent)*. One band: "This year: 2 novices · 1 solemn profession · 214 students · 64,000 Grotto pilgrims." It shows life and growth in a single line.
15. **A regular, light voice** *(Portsmouth's weekly "The Current" with "This Week in Heaven"; Gethsemani's "We welcome Malcolm Hayes to our community")*. Run a short weekly or monthly note with fixed columns (saints of the week, a Grotto miniature of the month, news from the Prep) that one monk can keep going. That keeps the site from going stale like Saint Vincent's.

Also worth taking: St. Michael's summer-camp-style **"A Typical Day" timetable** for the boarding school (6:30 Rise … 9:30 Lights out). It does more for parents than any brochure paragraph. Mount Angel's "**Come as an Individual / as a Group / for the Day**" fits Grotto visits (pilgrims, bus tours, school groups).

## Recommended top-level navigation

Header (desktop): **logo · Grotto · School · Monastery · Visit · Shop · ♥ Give**, with a small utility line above it: *Today: Grotto open 9–5 · Vespers 5:00 PM · Prayer request*.

- **Ave Maria Grotto**: The miniatures (an interactive map or index of all 125, each with a photo and its story), Br. Joseph Zoettl, Plan your visit (hours, tickets, groups, accessibility), History.
- **St. Bernard Prep**: About, Boarding life ("A Day at St. Bernard"), Admissions (Inquire / Visit / Apply), Academics & Athletics, plus a link to the school's full site if it stays separate. Keep the school's brand, but give its page here the same layout as the others.
- **Monastery**: Our monks, Prayer & schedule (with the live horarium and audio), Our life (a day in the life), History since 1891, Vocations, Oblates.
- **Visit**: Grotto hours, Mass and Office times, retreats and guests, directions. *Come as an individual / as a group / for the day.*
- **Shop**: the Grotto gift shop online, monastic goods, books.
- **Give** (a pill button): the annual appeal, Friends of the Grotto, the school fund, planned giving, Mass intentions.

On the phone: logo, hamburger, and a Give pill. The full-screen menu should show the four big words (Grotto, School, Monastery, Visit) the way St. Michael's shows its three.

## Recommended home-page order

1. **Hero:** a quiet photo or silent loop of the monks at Vespers (or a monk's hands restoring a miniature), the abbey's name, one sentence, and **today's hours pill** (Grotto / next prayer).
2. **"Two doors, one house":** two large equal cards, *Ave Maria Grotto: 125 miniature shrines built by one monk* and *St. Bernard Prep: Benedictine boarding and day school since 1891*, with a line under both: *Both are works of the monks of St. Bernard Abbey.* This replaces Subiaco's chooser page with something you can scroll past.
3. **Pray with us:** the live "now / next" horarium, a chant clip, and a link to the full schedule.
4. **Our monks:** the "A Teacher / A Grotto Keeper / A Beekeeper…" strip, linking to "Meet the monks".
5. **The Grotto story:** Br. Joseph Zoettl in a few sentences, one striking miniature photo, "Plan your visit", and three gift-shop items.
6. **The school story:** "A Day at St. Bernard", one alumni quote, "Visit / Apply".
7. **Three asks:** Let us pray for you → Is God calling you? → Be part of our mission.
8. **This year at St. Bernard:** the numbers band, then the latest two or three news items.
9. **Footer:** address, map link, hours, phone, newsletter sign-up, and safeguarding and other policy links.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- **Content that only appears with animation** (St. Michael's, Saint Meinrad, Clear Creek, Portsmouth School): if the fade-in script doesn't run, or the reader has reduced motion on, whole sections are blank. Render content visible by default and animate it gently on top.
- **Dated widgets that announce neglect:** Saint Vincent's December-2025 homilies, Conception's "There are no upcoming events", Gethsemani's © 2022, Portsmouth's June fiscal-year pop-up still showing in November. Anything with a date on the home page needs an owner or must hide itself when empty.
- **Interruptions:** a donation pop-up over the hero, a cookie wall and a chat bot at once (Portsmouth School). A monastery site should feel like walking into the church, not a car dealership.
- **A splash page that makes you choose** (Subiaco). It's an extra click, and anyone who doesn't fit a side (a pilgrim, a donor, a parent who wants Mass times) is stuck.
- **Shop takeover** (Clear Creek's huge mega-menu, product grids on the home page). Keep the shop one click away.
- **Two unrelated brands** for school and monastery (Portsmouth). Share type, colour and photography even if the school keeps its own crest.
- **Rows of portraits with no stories** (St. Michael's) and **the opposite, monks hidden entirely** (Clear Creek). For St. Bernard, faces plus a few words from each monk are what make it human.
- **Giving that leaves the site** for another domain (Saint Meinrad sends you to the seminary's site). Keep the donation form on the abbey's own site, or an embedded form like St. Michael's Donorbox, and match its look.
- **Low contrast** (gold on cream at St. Michael's, small grey text at Mount Angel and Gethsemani) and **24-hour times** (Saint Louis Abbey's "1730"). Write "5:30 PM".
- **Leaked working pages** in the menus (Portsmouth's "SG – Athletics", "Campaign Leadership – old"), and **broken links** (Saint Meinrad's Monk Directory goes to a 404). Check every link before launch.
- **Carousels hiding the main point** (Mount Angel's benefit dinner as the hero, Subiaco's number carousels, Christ in the Desert's 14-slide slideshow). Choose one image and one message.
