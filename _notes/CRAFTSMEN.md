## 1. Brother Joseph Zoettl, O.S.B. (1878–1961), builder of the Ave Maria Grotto

**Spelling:** The Grotto's home page, the NRHP nomination's biography and most sources write "Zoettl". The Grotto's About page and its Guide write "Zoetl". The NRHP form's Section 7 also writes "Zoetl". The abbey's home page writes "Zoetle". The statue's inscription reads "Zoettl" (Highsmith photo, LOC 2010640619).

### Life

- **Birth.** Born 1878 in Landshut, Bavaria (Grotto/About; Grotto Guide; NRHP; EOA says "Bavaria"). Baptized Michael Zoettl in St. Martin's Church, Landshut (SPACES; Grotto Guide: "Saint Martin's Church—where Brother Joseph was baptized in 1878"). Grotto Guide: "patron of Brother Joseph, whose baptismal name was Michael."
  - Date: **24 January 1878** (Necrology). He opens his own sketch with: "About three days before Pope Pius IX died, I was born near the Trausnitz Castle in Landshut, Bavaria" (Necrology).
  - **Disagreement:** Pius IX died on 7 February 1878, so "about three days before" points to early February, not 24 January. Ask the monks whether the abbey archives hold his baptismal record.
- **Family.** His mother died when he was young, and his stepmother decided he should become a priest (NRHP). His father worked in a brewery (Necrology). In his own words: "Why my stepmother wanted me to become a priest I have no inkling. I was not pious, in fact religion became somewhat a burden" (Necrology). He nearly died of the flu in 1891 (Necrology).
- **Coming to St. Bernard, 1892.** Fr. Gamelbert Brunner came to Europe in January 1892 looking for candidates for the abbey, and young Michael decided to go (Necrology). "January 27, 1892 was the date of departure. All night I cried till we entered the railroad car, no homesickness, but what homesickness is, I found out many years later in Stonega, Virginia" (his words, Necrology).
  - He arrived at the abbey aged 14 to study for the priesthood (NRHP; SPACES; Grotto/About gives "In 1892 he came to the newly founded St. Bernard Abbey"). EOA says he was "recruited in his home country by a visiting American priest." The Georgia Bulletin, citing the film, says he came through Ellis Island in 1892.
  - **Small disagreement:** Two 1950s ADAH photograph captions say he was "13 years of age" (https://digital.archives.alabama.gov/digital/collection/photo/id/15067 and /15070).
- **The injury and the brotherhood.** After three years of classes the abbot told him he could not become a priest because of a physical disability: he "had been injured in an accident at the abbey which left him a hunchback" (NRHP). He "gladly agreed to a life as a lay brother" (NRHP). He took vows in 1897 (SPACES; Morning News; Wikipedia).
  - **How he was hurt, as the sources tell it:**
    - NRHP says only "an accident at the abbey".
    - Morning News: "As a student Brother Joe had helped in the construction of the abbey buildings, during which time he suffered a back injury."
    - Georgia Bulletin (from the documentary): "While working in the belfry of the abbey church, which the monks were building themselves, Zoettl was severely injured in an accident that aggravated an existing back condition."
    - Wikipedia: "maimed in an accident that left him slightly hunched due to cervical kyphosis" and "not allowed to be ordained … due to the rule … that any man with a distracting disability could not be ordained" (its source is the Morning News).
    - NRHP: "A severe back injury prevented him from completing his studies and being ordained."
- **Mission years.** He kept house for Benedictine priests in the missions (NRHP: "spent the next several years keeping house for various Benedictine fathers in the Southeast"). The Necrology names Tuscumbia and Decatur, Alabama; Dayton, Tennessee; and Pocahontas and Stonega, Virginia. Of one priest he wrote: "He called my cooking poison, he would also examine the coal ashes to see how much coal was in there then he would call me down" (Necrology).
  - The Necrology notice for Fr. Robert Reitmeier (1873–1956) says "The famous Br. Joseph Zoettl was his housekeeper and catechist at Stonega, Virginia"; Fr. Robert was serving Virginia and Kentucky parishes by 1903.
  - His first grotto, built of rocks around a $24 concrete statue, was dedicated in 1905. "By his own account, he had built several similar ones while serving in Virginia" (NRHP).
  - He returned to St. Bernard in 1908 and left it again only once, for a trip to Birmingham (NRHP). The Georgia Bulletin, citing the film, mentions trips to Virginia and Tuscumbia.
- **The powerhouse.**
  - NRHP: "In 1910 he was placed in charge of the boilers in the new abbey power house, a job that he kept until he retired. It was a job that required his attention around the clock yet left much free time." SPACES also gives 1910.
  - Necrology: "put in charge of the powerhouse built in 1911. Long, burdensome workdays of seventeen hours … For thirty years this was his routine." His words: "It became very tedious, for I had to pump from morning to night every day, Sunday included. To pass time, I started hobbies again."
  - Grotto/About: "When not busy shoveling coal into the furnaces, Brother took time to construct some miniature buildings."
  - **Small disagreement:** 1910 (NRHP, SPACES) or 1911 (Necrology).
  - His first powerhouse hobby was a model train. Its engine was tied by a string to a flywheel that generated electricity, and silver wires on the smokestack trembled to look like smoke (NRHP; SPACES).
  - He built his own miniature of the abbey Power House, dated 1953 (NRHP sketch-map legend item 9). Wikipedia also notes it.
- **The little grottoes.** "One day Father Dominic [Downs] came to me with some little statues and to see if I could make small grottoes, he had a store in front of the college and sold religious articles to help missions. When I had made two grottoes I thought that would be all, but as Father Dominic sold them right away, he always brought more statues and it became a regular business" (his words, Necrology).
  - NRHP: he set up a worktable in the boiler room and made hand-sized grottoes (and ashtrays) for the abbey gift shop. He "completed 5000 of these before he quit counting although he continued to turn them out for another twenty years."
  - The Necrology also gives "five thousand small grottoes".
  - The Georgia Bulletin's version, from the film, is that a monk received 500 statues of the Virgin Mary.
- **Little Jerusalem.** His words: "in 1918 … I began working with cement. The first thing I constructed was a church, that is still in existence, then a year later I began with some small oriental buildings which were later called Little Jerusalem" (Necrology).
  - An ADAH photo caption (1950s, Alabama State Chamber of Commerce) shows him "standing by Roman Catacombs, and his first large Church, which were built in 1918."
  - The pieces stood on the brothers' recreation ground (NRHP; EOA) or in "the gardens near the monastery" (Grotto/About). The guest master, Fr. Lawrence O'Leary, took visitors to see them, "but by and by other people came and it became a real nuisance which could not be stopped anymore" (his words, Necrology).
  - **When he began, as the sources tell it:**
    - "about 1912" (Grotto/About; Grotto Guide)
    - "ca. 1915" or "probably about 1915-18" (NRHP)
    - "around 1918" (EOA)
    - 1918 in his own words (Necrology)
    - Little Jerusalem dated "1915-25" (NRHP map legend) and "c1915-30" (SPACES)
- **The move to the quarry, 1932–34.** NRHP: the abandoned rock quarry "was transformed between 1932 and 1934," and the buildings stayed on the recreation ground "until 1932 when they were transferred to the quarry." His words: "… in July 1932 when I came back I told Abbot Bernard I was getting old and could hardly do much anymore but he would not listen, so I started and had plenty to do, never dreamed I would get so old" (Necrology).
  - Grotto/About says the pieces "were moved to the present site in 1934."
  - The Georgia Bulletin (film) says the abbot first refused the quarry and changed his mind about 12 years later. It also says that in 1933 "a train wreck left a load of damaged Alabama marble" that was given to him.
  - The quarry: "a former stone quarry used by the Abbey" (Grotto/About). EOA calls it "the nearby limestone quarry where the blocks for Saint Bernard Abbey's buildings were hewn." See the stone disagreement in section 3.
- **Opening and dedication, 1934.** Opened to the public and dedicated on **17 May 1934** (SPACES: "1934 May 17th. Ave Maria Grotto was opened to the public and dedicated"; Alabama News Center "On this day" item dated 17 May: https://alabamanewscenter.com/2017/05/17/91511). The site took its name from Zoettl's largest piece, "The Ave Maria Grotto" (EOA).
  - Abbey site: "In 1934 the Ave Maria Grotto … was dedicated in honor of the Blessed Virgin Mary."
  - The main grotto is 27 ft high, 27 ft wide and 27 ft deep (Grotto Guide; EOA). NRHP says "27 feet high and nearly as wide."
  - Its concrete shell "was constructed by local masons to Br. Joe's specifications." The ceiling was hung with railroad spikes and chicken wire, which he made into stalactites with concrete, glass, marbles and stones (NRHP). Some stalactites are marble from the Gantt quarry, Sylacauga (EOA).
  - He built the altar of crushed glass, stones and cement (Grotto Guide).
  - The statues are Our Lady (Grotto Guide: "Our Lady of Prompt Succor"; NRHP: "Our Lady Queen of the Universe"), St. Benedict and St. Scholastica.
  - The stone shelter of the Bethlehem Nativity was also not built by him (NRHP).
- **Who landscaped the quarry.** NRHP and SPACES both say "**Br.** Patrick O'Neill" landscaped the four-acre site in 1932–34. He laid out the circular path and planted shrubs, trees and grass.
  - The Necrology calls him "**Father** Patrick O'Neill … the monk most responsible for the landscaping." Its own notice for Fr. Patrick O'Neill (1887–1971) shows he was a priest, ordained in 1915.
  - **Disagreement on his title:** the Necrology's "Father" is the better-sourced.
- **How he built.**
  - He worked at a table, made each part separately, then assembled them on site. His tools were simple: hammers, pliers, brushes, scissors and flatware. Color was mixed into the cement (NRHP; SPACES).
  - He worked mostly "from small, two-dimensional views on picture postcards and without dimensions" (NRHP). Of all his replicas he had seen only about six, those in Landshut and at St. Bernard (Grotto/About; EOA). He took ideas from "extensive readings of history and the Bible" (Grotto/About).
  - **Materials** (NRHP): marbles, seashells, costume jewelry, bits of glass, bottles and cold-cream jars, tiles, dinner plates, toilet-tank floats, old watches, small figurines, bicycle reflectors and rocks. Grotto/About adds broken plates, ceramic tile and beads. Many materials were donated by visitors; "he scrounged the rest" (NRHP).
  - **Examples:**
    - Toilet-tank floats form the twin domes of the Mobile cathedral (NRHP).
    - An old birdcage is the dome of St. Peter's, built 1954 (Grotto Guide).
    - The Temple of the Fairies uses more than a dozen cold-cream jars (Grotto Guide).
    - The Guardian Angel grotto has a blue cross made of ink bottles (Grotto Guide).
    - The Tower of Thanks is topped with Irish fishing-net floats. It thanks the friends who sent him materials (Grotto Guide).
- **Number of works.**
  - **125** (Grotto/About; EOA; Grotto Guide).
  - "approximately **150**" (NRHP; SPACES writes "125/150" and asks "How many monuments are there 125 or 150 or??").
  - Wikipedia: "some 50 years" of work. EOA: "over 40 years". NRHP: "more than 85 percent of the present works were in place by 1940 and … almost 95 percent were completed by 1950."
  - Park size: four acres (Grotto/Home; NRHP) or "more than three acres" (EOA).
- **His last work.** The Lourdes Basilica, 1958, when he was 80 (Grotto/About; Grotto Guide; EOA). His health then made further work impossible (NRHP).
- **Character.** "A small, fragile man, barely five feet tall and weighing less than 100 pounds … He avoided publicity and tended the Grotto early in the morning and late in the afternoon when he would not encounter tourists" (NRHP). The NRHP also says he "was reluctant even to take credit for the Grotto." His favorite saint was St. Thérèse of Lisieux (Grotto gallery page, https://www.avemariagrotto.com/gallery.html).
- **Death and burial.** Died **15 October 1961** (EOA; Necrology) and was buried in the abbey cemetery (Grotto/About; EOA), "in a special bronze coffin" (Necrology). Visitors are welcome at the grave, 100 yards north of the Grotto gift shop (Grotto/About).
- **Recognition.**
  - Alabama Register of Landmarks and Heritage, 24 Feb 1976 (Wikipedia).
  - National Register of Historic Places, 19 Jan 1984, "Alabama's first nomination of a folk art environment" (SPACES; Wikipedia). The nomination calls him "an exceptionally talented vernacular artist and craftsman."
- **Later stewards (laymen, not monks).** Leo Schwaiger (born 1925) cared for the Grotto from 1963 to September 2014. He added the Basilica of Knock, Our Lady of Guadalupe, the Great Wall of China, the Pyramids, Montserrat and the Shrine of Our Lady of Good Help (Grotto Guide).
- **Bronze statue of Br. Joseph** at the Grotto, by sculptor **Ira Chaffin**, with a model of St. Martin's, Landshut at its feet.
  - Bhamwiki gives 2007 (https://www.bhamwiki.com/w/Joseph_Zoettl). A Pixeo photo guide gives 2009. Both reached only through a search summary; Bhamwiki blocks scripted reading.
  - **LEAD:** confirm the date with the abbey.
- **Books and film to consult.**
  - John Morris, *Miniature Miracle* (Huntsville: Honeysuckle Imprint, 1991) (cited by EOA).
  - Rev. Aloysius Plaisance, OSB, *An Illustrated Self Guide of the Ave Maria Grotto* (cited by SPACES and the Morning News).
  - Linda Bayer, "Ave Maria Grotto nominated," *The Preservation Report* (Alabama Historical Commission, Nov–Dec 1983) (SPACES).
  - George Collins et al., *Fantastic Architecture* (1980); Jan Wampler, *All Their Own* (1977); Seymour Rosen, *In Celebration of Ourselves* (1979) (NRHP).
  - Cliff Vaughn (Red Clay Pictures), documentary *Brother Joseph and the Grotto* (premiered 24 Oct 2013 at the abbey) (Georgia Bulletin; DVD listed in the Grotto shop).
  - A Paramount newsreel, "Unusual Occupations," is the only surviving film of him at work (Georgia Bulletin).
  - An Ernie Pyle column of 1939 described the Grotto (NRHP map-legend note).

## 3. Other craftsman monks of St. Bernard Abbey

**About the Necrology:** Most of the historical names below come from the abbey's own Necrology (see the key). It is the abbey's authoritative record, and it is public.

### Well sourced

**Woodworkers, carpenters, builders**

- **Br. Gerard Staudinger** (b. 5 Mar 1880, Deggendorf, Bavaria; d. 1963). Apprenticed as a carpenter by age 15, came to St. Bernard in 1906, first vows 1907, and had "a long and industrious career as carpenter and builder at St. Bernard."
  - He ran "the old steam-powered lumber mill."
  - In 1914 his carpenter shop made 250 oak dining chairs, the refectory tables and 300 desks.
  - He made the pews in the Abbey Church and the communion railing in the old Students' Chapel.
  - He was Dean of the Brothers from 1922, and was cellarer and "Braumeister" (brewer).
  - *Source: Necrology.*
  - **Discrepancy:** the Necrology dates the Abbey Church pews "(1977)", which cannot be right for a man who died in 1963. It is probably a typo; the church was built in the building campaign begun in 1957 (see Abbot Bede below).
  - **LEAD:** the Grotto shop sells "St. Bernard Classroom Chair … Antique classroom chairs used at St. Bernard College and Prep school. Solid oak construction" (https://www.avemariagrotto.com/store/p36/St._Bernard_Classroom_Chair.html). Ask whether these are from Br. Gerard's shop.
- **Br. Nicholas Joseph Weiss** (b. 1907, Bavaria; came 1925, first vows 1926; d. 1982). "Worked as a skilled cabinet maker in the Abbey shops … trained in the art of woodworking and had a natural talent for wood carving."
  - His works include "glorious sculptures, among the finest of which is an Altar of Repose used in the liturgical services of Holy Week here at St. Bernard."
  - He carved a crosier from magnolia wood for Bishop Joseph Vath of Birmingham, and made "the great, handsome Abbot's table in our community refectory," his last work.
  - He kept ducks, geese and swans.
  - His brother was Br. Placidus Weiss and his uncle Fr. Bonaventure Winklmann.
  - *Source: Necrology.*
  - → **This is the documented "Br. Nicholas" among the abbey's craftsmen.** The campus map used by the Bloomin' Festival site is credited to a "Br. Nicholas" (`bloominfestival` repo). That drawing is recent, so it is presumably by a different, living monk. **LEAD:** ask the community who drew it, and whether there is a living Br. or Fr. Nicholas.
- **Br. Ambrose Holzner** (b. 1881, Übersee, Bavaria; d. 1901). "He had been a very diligent worker in the carpenter shop." *Necrology.*
- **Fr. Jerome Lawrence** (b. 1879, Irishtown, Pa.; d. 1937). "Learned in his youth the carpenter's trade."
  - He "drew the plans for and superintended the erection of the old St. Bernard College Gym. He actually built the three handball courts."
  - He dammed the gully to make the swimming pond, and built two tennis courts and a half-mile track.
  - *Necrology.*
- **Fr. Placidus Becker** (came 1892 from Stukenbrock, Westphalia; ordained 1900; d. 1948). A fellow scholastic of Br. Joseph Zoettl.
  - He studied art at St. Anselm's, taught art, and ran "the pumping engine."
  - "St. Bernard's many beautiful trees show the mark of his hand … he laid out many of the landscapes of St. Bernard."
  - He "drew up the plans for the first independent monastery building of St. Bernard."
  - He was best known for his painting, including a reproduction of Da Vinci's "Last Supper" for the old monastic refectory.
  - *Necrology.*
- **Fr. Bonaventure Winklmann** (b. 1884, Marzling, Bavaria; d. 1925). Had "a keen mechanical mind" and studied architectural engineering and drafting. "He designed the monuments and coat-of-arms and made the blueprints for the barns and stables." *Necrology.*
- **Abbot Bede Luibel** (1916–1986), 5th abbot. Not a craftsman himself, but the builder-abbot: "Starting in 1957, a building campaign added Boniface Hall, the Abbey Church, the dining hall and the faculty house (monastery)" (*Necrology*).
  - The abbey's renovation page gives the monastery building as "Built in 1963" (https://www.stbernardabbey.com/monastery-renovation).
  - He commissioned German sculptor **Herbert Jogerst** (a layman) "to carve the statues in our Abbey Church" (Br. Jude obituary, below).
  - An ADAH postcard says the Abbey Church "is constructed of native sandstone quarried on the monastery grounds" (https://digital.archives.alabama.gov/digital/collection/photo/id/22277).
  - **LEAD:** the architect of the Abbey Church and its exact dedication date were not found in any source. Check with the abbey archives.

**Artists, sculptors, painters, illuminators**

- **Br. Jude (Henry Odie) Johnston, Jr.** (b. 19 Jan 1933, Florence, Ala.; d. 19 Jun 2025). The last lay brother of St. Bernard.
  - Life:
    - St. Bernard Prep class of 1951; novitiate 1953; first profession 15 Aug 1954; perpetual simple vows 1957.
    - Early on he made habits and vestments in the tailor shop. He then studied ceramics in the "mud room" with Fr. Timothy Harrison.
    - Abbot Bede apprenticed him to sculptor Herbert Jogerst. During that apprenticeship he carved a **bust of Br. Joseph Zoettl**, "which can still be seen today in the Grotto Shop"; he called it "my first awkward attempt at portraiture."
    - He studied at Notre Dame, Auburn and Yale "in the fields of sculpture, photography, engineering, architecture, and artistic design," and taught at Yale and at Southern Benedictine College.
  - His carved wooden **Dove of the Holy Spirit** ("Veni Sancte Spiritus") hangs over the Chapter Room door.
  - *Sources:* abbey obituary in the Cullman Tribune, 23 Jun 2025 (https://www.cullmantribune.com/2025/06/23/obituary-henry-odie-johnston-jr/) and One Voice, Diocese of Birmingham (https://onevoicebhm.org/last-lay-brother-st-bernard-abbey-peacefully-goes-home-lord).
  - He worked under the name **"Nov Ontos"** ("new being"). He left his collection to the Evelyn Burrow Museum, Wallace State Community College, which held an exhibition in 2026 of his drawings, architectural forms and CADD designs. The museum director calls him "a true polymath—a brilliant scholar of architecture, engineering, sculpture, and art" (https://www.wallacestate.edu/news/2026/04/01/jude-johnston_exhibit.html; https://www.cullmantribune.com/?p=130759).
  - → **This is the "artist and engineer" Br. Jude the webmaster remembered.** Well sourced.
  - **LEAD:** a CBS 42 article (https://www.cbs42.com/news/local/for-70-years-jude-johnston-devoted-his-spiritual-life-to-st-bernard-abbey-but-the-alabama-monk-was-also-committed-to-his-art/) is said to report that he studied sculpture at Notre Dame under Ivan Meštrović by 1961. The page was blocked to me; this comes only from a search summary.
- **Fr. Timothy Harrison** (b. 1921, Birmingham; came 1935; ordained 1948; d. 3 Jul 1982).
  - "Painting, sculpture, pottery, music, and the theater arts were all his métier." He trained at St. Bernard College, St. Benedict's (Atchison), Notre Dame, Catholic University and the Dante Alighieri Institute, Florence (*Necrology*).
  - He made the **Statue of Christ** at the Grotto, "who said that the concrete was difficult to work with and preferred to let Brother Joseph handle the building structures" (Grotto Guide).
  - He was Br. Jude's ceramics teacher (Br. Jude obituary).
- **Fr. Edward Stoeberl** (b. 20 Oct 1882, Landshut; came 1894; ordained 1908; d. 1946). Studied music at Ottobeuron from 1910 and collected chant melodies, which "he painstakingly copied into huge choir books which he illustrated gloriously. They can still be seen today." He was choirmaster and ran the Abbey farm, planting the vineyards for Mass wine. *Necrology.* → A scribe and illuminator. **LEAD:** photograph these choir books.
- **Fr. Bede Knapke** (1886–1937). A botanist and photographer who set up a darkroom. *Necrology.* A minor craft.

**Printers and publishers**

- **Fr. Charles Fries** (b. 1872, Kittanning, Pa.; d. 1935). Was an apprentice printer at the *Kittanning News*. At St. Bernard he "remodeled an old printing press that had already seen years of service at St. Vincent's, and, after setting the type by hand, printed the yearly catalogs for the college as well as various stationery items." *Necrology.*
- **The St. Bernard Scriptorium** (current): the abbey's imprint, now bringing back into print books by **Fr. Aloysius Plaisance** (1920–1992):
  - *The Dear Editor Monk*
  - *An Alabama Monk in the Holy Land*
  - *An Alabama Monk Bicycles the British Isles*
  - *An Alabama Monk Hikes the Smokies*
  - *Seeing Europe After 40 Years*

  It also publishes Abbot Ambrose Reger's *The Benedictines in Alabama* (https://www.avemariagrotto.com/store/c14/St._Bernard_Scriptorium.html). The abbey's home page lists "publishing" among the monks' crafts.
  - Fr. Aloysius also wrote the Grotto's illustrated self-guide. As Grotto director he "set up a hobby shop to make items to be sold in the Grotto gift shop … cutting small saplings to make walking sticks … Bird houses and feline houses" (*Necrology*).
  - Abbot Marcus Voss "published a four-volume 'Book of Hours' for use in the monastic choir" (Cullman Times, https://cullmantimes.com/?p=271209).

**Glaziers, shoemakers, tailors, rosary-makers**

- **Br. Vincent Kiefl** (b. 1886, Deggendorf; professed 1907; d. 1970). Trained by the Mercy Sisters as a glazier and baker, and worked as the abbey's glazier until old age. *Necrology.*
- **Br. Edmund Benziger** (b. 1898, Sappenfeld, Bavaria; vows 1925; drowned 4 Jul 1934). The community's shoemaker. *Necrology.*
- **Br. Wolfgang Wolf** (b. 1872, Regensburg; d. 1950). Was the abbey's tailor before becoming a botanist of world reputation (his collection is now at Auburn University). *Necrology.*
- **Br. Clement Kantz** (1889–1969). Worked in the garden, kitchen and tailor shop. *Necrology.*
- **Br. Aemilian Kopps** (b. 1849, Visbek, Oldenburg; d. 1926). Head gardener from 1904. In old age he made rosaries "not only [for] all the monks … but also … whole mission districts." *Necrology.*

**Engineers, plant men, mechanics**

- **Br. Francis Rotkopf** (b. 1904, Elberfeld-Barmen; vows 1924; d. 3 Dec 1984). Kept "the boilers" and General Maintenance after Br. Joseph, "resourceful and inventive in physical plant husbandry." The Necrology calls him "the last of those great men, our native German brothers."
- **Fr. Ronald Veigl** (1921–1970). An Army radar and radio specialist; "opened the first radio station in Cullman and was licensed as a radio engineer by the F.C.C." *Necrology.*
- **Br. John Paul Brunschwyler** (1920–2010). Had worked as an engineer in West Virginia. At the abbey he kept the car fleet. *Necrology.*

**Farmers, gardeners, landscapers**

- **Fr. Patrick O'Neill** (1887–1971). Landscaped the Grotto quarry in 1932–34 (Necrology; NRHP and SPACES call him "Br.").
- **Fr. Dominic Downs** (1880–1974). Sold Br. Joseph's first grottoes in his store. Later he "grew the most gorgeous flowers in our gardens" and helped Grotto guests. *Necrology.*
- **Br. Norbert Knapke** (1846–1933). Worked the farm and the hennery. *Necrology.*
- **Fr. Edward Stoeberl.** The Abbey farm and vineyards (above).
- **Br. Charles Manning** (vows 1971, living). "Enjoyed working on the campus grounds and landscaping, planting a number of trees on campus." He is now the abbey's procurator (Cullman Times, https://cullmantimes.com/?p=271209; abbey monk profiles).
- **Abbot Brendan J. Seipel** (elected 11th abbot 29 Jul 2026). Was earlier "Director of the Abbey Farm" (abbey home page).
- **Abbey Byre:** the abbey's old barn, used now for events (abbey dedication page). **LEAD:** a wedding-venue listing says the monks built it in 1920 as a hay barn for their dairy herd and that it keeps "original wood milled at St. Bernard"; that listing was seen only in a search summary.

**Bakers (current)**

- **Br. Benedict** (came 2007): "I currently serve as head baker of the monastery." **Fr. Pachomius** (came 2014): "In the past, I was in charge of the bakery." *Abbey monk profiles, https://www.stbernardabbey.com/monk-profiles.*
- The abbey's breads are sold as "St. Bernard's Best": Classic Recipe (cinnamon raisin), Monte Cassino, and Monk's Ration ("named after St. Benedict's recommendation for a 'monk's ration' in his Rule") (Grotto shop).
- The abbey home page: "St. Bernard monks practice many crafts and manual arts to help support the monastery. These include: baking, farming, tailoring, publishing, gardening, landscaping, and more."

**Current woodshop**

- **St. Bernard Abbey Woodworks**: "a ministry of the Benedictine monks … We specialize in custom-made furniture, altars, pulpits, pews, doors, cabinets, and more. We also offer restoration and refinishing." Contact carpenter@stbernardabbey.com (https://www.stbernardabbey.com/woodworks).
  - The abbey also sells engraved **wooden columbarium boxes** for the graveyard chapel (https://www.stbernardabbey.com/columbarium).
  - **LEAD:** the woodworks page names no monks. Ask who works in the shop today, and get photos of their work.

### Leads to check with the monks

- Who drew the campus map credited to "Br. Nicholas" in the Bloomin' Festival site, and is there a living Br. or Fr. Nicholas? (The only Br. Nicholas in the records is Nicholas Weiss, d. 1982.)
- Who built the abbey's stone buildings? Did monks quarry and cut the stone, and which ones? Sources say only that the Grotto quarry supplied the abbey's building stone (EOA) and that the church is "native sandstone quarried on the monastery grounds" (ADAH postcard).
  - **Stone disagreement:** EOA says the quarry was **limestone**. The ADAH postcard says the church is **sandstone**, and the NRHP calls the Nativity shelter a "sandstone building." The Necrology (Br. Wolfgang) says the campus lies "in a sand stone region."
- The architect and builders of the 1950s–60s Abbey Church, and which of its furnishings are monk-made: Br. Gerard's pews; Jogerst's statues are by a layman.
- Organ builders, iconographers, blacksmiths and metalworkers: **nothing found**. The Grotto shop has an "Icons" category, but there is no evidence that monks write icons. Ask.
- Br. Joseph's autobiographical sketch: where is the original?
- Statue of Br. Joseph by Ira Chaffin: the date (2007 or 2009) and who holds its rights.
- Br. Placidus Weiss (brother of Br. Nicholas): his own Necrology notice was not read. Do not confuse him with **Br. Placidus Schiesser** (b. 1851, Bavaria; d. 1901), a founder, who "with the exception of one year as gardener … was the baker at the monastery" (Necrology): the abbey's first recorded baker.
